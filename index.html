The Way We See

No wedding unfolds in a straight line.

A distracted child.

Someone laughing in the background.

A fleeting glance nobody else noticed.

Conversations crossing over each other.

Tiny details hidden inside larger moments.

These are the things we pay attention to. Not just what happened — but how life moved through it. While one moment is happening in the centre, five others are quietly unfolding around it. We are watching all of them — less interested in performances, and more interested in what people reveal when they forget the camera is there. That unpredictability is what keeps us curious, and what keeps the photographs alive long after the day is over.

Our Philosophy

Why One Thousand Tales?

Photography can become a document of human behaviour and family history — not just photographs to look at, but photographs people return to for years.

01

Observation over Direction

We never direct people or create polished emotional scenes. We observe. The best moments happen when no one is watching the camera.

02

Emotion in its Natural State

Some photographs are emotional. Some are chaotic. Some are funny without trying to be. We don't push emotions — we catch them in the wild.

03

Images That Stay Alive

A gesture in the background. A relationship hidden within the frame. A detail that once felt invisible. Our images continue revealing new things over time.

04

Everyone in the Frame

One Thousand Tales was built around the entire story — where everyone becomes part of the frame, not just the couple at the centre.

The Storyteller

Not just a photographer.
A student of human moments.

Dilip Kumar

I've always been more interested in what happens around the moment than the moment itself. The things people miss. The brief interactions that disappear almost immediately after they happen.

What started as a passion for photography slowly became a realisation — that weddings are not just grand celebrations. They are made up of moments that will never exist in the same way again.

A father's glance. A mother holding back tears. A quiet smile between two people before everything begins.

Over time, I realized I wasn't interested in directing people. What interested me more was unpredictability — the way emotions shift without warning, the strange humour that appears in serious situations.
